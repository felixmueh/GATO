TIAGo horizon, discretization, and controller-clock pilot (2026-09-24)
==================================================================

Status and interpretation
-------------------------
This is a bounded development screen, not a final parameter-selection study.
The paired matrix contains all 18 requested task-cells (36 selected B1/B16
outputs). It separates duration from grid refinement. The additional exact
dt=.03 probes extend to 3.81 seconds on task 1. No hard cell was removed or
replaced with a shorter motion. Latency is measured separately and never changes
physical simulation time. The independent batching audit is reported separately;
full N16 and preliminary N32 results do not certify all larger shapes.

Here feasible includes <20 mm final position error and physical bounds; final
tool speed is checked separately. Physical means no sampled tool-center cylinder
collision, and no joint-position, velocity, or effort violation. The additional
8 mm optimization padding is a soft cost, not a hard clearance guarantee.

All 36 short prefixes are safe in these checks, with maximum tool-position
prediction error .725 mm. RK4 step halving from 1 ms to .5 ms changes prefix
terminal joint velocity by at most 2.90e-10 rad/s. This validates short integration
intervals; failed 1.8 s full rollouts have much larger refinement differences
(up to .105 rad/s) and are not claimed to be equally well resolved.

These warm runs
all remain physically safe. N128/1.8 is NOT settled at the unchanged 20 mm
criterion, despite being close and moving slowly. N32/.45 and all three N64/.9
clock settings settle. All these trajectories slightly enter the soft padding
but retain >7 mm physical cylinder clearance. Longer open-loop torque drift
therefore does not imply failure under frequent measured-state feedback.
Cold reset also settles safely, with 5.78 mm minimum physical clearance and
executed cost .87377 versus warm .84986. Its smaller endpoint error does not
mean lower integrated cost. Unlike the initial matrix, feedback solves sometimes
set their local KKT flag: 7/20 N32/.45, 8/20 N64/.9, and 9/20 N128/1.8 warm
steps at clock.06. N128's full torque replay remains infeasible throughout;
local stopping criteria therefore do not certify full shooting fidelity.

Exact dt=.03, task 1/B1: N32/.93 has feasible coarse but infeasible fine replay;
N64/1.89 and N128/3.81 fail both full replays. Small local transcription defects
can coexist with explosive full torque-shooting errors. The N64/1.8 exact-input
rescue with 800 SQP iterations accepts [122,0,0,0] steps per 200-iteration pass;
quadrupling PCG cap to 4000 accepts [168,0] at 400 SQP. Neither repairs full replay.
Planned local defects are only order 1e-5 rad/rad/s, so propagation sensitivity
is an important finding alongside stalled line searches and PCG caps. No tested
matrix winner reaches the solver's KKT stopping condition. CPU reference results
in the JSON are discrete, independently optimized comparators, not GATO results
or continuous reachability certificates.

Recommendation for discussion: N64/.9 is a defensible next development baseline
with a .06 s measured-state controller clock; N128/1.8 remains a valuable stress
case, not a validated long-horizon optimum. This pilot does not prove that .9 s
is the best horizon, that long horizons are intrinsically unusable, or that the
old .45 s choice was justified. The old choice primarily stabilized development
and affordable tuning; adaptive-rho/low-budget failures were insufficient evidence
against longer horizons. Before expanding scenes, retain this distinction and
review the larger-shape batching gate. The portable suite's exact dt=.015 protocol
is separately specified and must not be described as already validated by these
near-.015 paired cells. No new scene family or configuration fuzzing was run.
N256/3.6 and a finer .03 s controller-clock test were not run in this bounded
pilot; the portable runner can extend those axes after review.

Declared objective and physics
------------------------------
T=(N-1)dt, s=dt/.03. Objective:
 .5 sum(k=0..N-2) [2s ||tool(q_k)-goal||^2 + .0001s ||u_k||^2
                   + s cylinder_residual(q_k)^2]
 + .5*.01s sum(k=0..N-1) ||v_k||^2
 + 100 ||tool(q_last)-goal||^2 + .5 cylinder_residual(q_last)^2.
Cylinder residual=max(1-distance_xy^2/(radius+.008)^2,0). Velocity at the final
knot is an O(dt) running-quadrature endpoint contribution; there is NO fixed
terminal velocity penalty. This is a declared consistent limiting objective,
not the frozen multimodal campaign's original objective. Continuous executed
cost uses duration-aware trapezoidal state quadrature and exact ZOH effort
integration, with the same coefficients and fixed terminal terms.

The optimization plant uses CUDA constant-acceleration updates. Independent
validation integrates [qdot=v, vdot=Pinocchio ABA(q,v,u)] in float64 RK4, with
exact ZOH control switches and exact controller boundaries. Initial states are
aligned to saved float32 CUDA values for integration comparisons; actual MPC
plant state remains float64, with measurement quantization recorded. RK4 is an
independent numerical reference, not a different robot model. Checks cover the
seven-joint arm/tool center only; no full-body, self-collision, hardware, or
real-time safety claim is made.

Reproducibility and limits
--------------------------
Exact tasks, qgoal, initial XU/reference arrays and hashes are saved in baseline
NPZ/input_manifest.json; --inputs-root replays them without IK regeneration.
Both rescue initial arrays were checked bit-identical to baseline. Per-cell
source/binary/URDF hashes, git provenance, machine/toolchain metadata, solver
telemetry and failures remain in raw summaries. Early baseline rows predate the
local-defect telemetry addition; these missing measurements are left missing.
MPC used tiago_horizon_mpc.py from commit1e65588; it was unchanged throughout the
six runs. The compact JSON preserves summary hashes and metrics. Full raw NPZ
artifacts are local, ignored outputs and require a separate archive for transfer;
a git checkout alone is not an exact-input archival package.
